# Rules and Principles of Visual Composition

## Fundamental Composition Rules

1. **Rule of Thirds** – Divide the canvas into a 3×3 grid and position important elements along the lines or intersections.
2. **Leading Lines** – Use lines, edges and shapes to guide the viewer's eye towards important elements.
3. **Negative Space** – Use empty space to create breathing room and emphasise subjects.
4. **Symmetry** – Arrange elements evenly around a central axis to create balance and order.
5. **Asymmetrical Balance** – Balance different visual elements through their relative size, colour, position and visual weight.
6. **Framing** – Use surrounding elements to frame and draw attention to the subject.
7. **Depth and Layering** – Create depth using foreground, middle ground and background.
8. **Golden Ratio** – Use proportional relationships to organise elements harmoniously.
9. **Golden Spiral** – Arrange elements along a spiral to guide the viewer's attention.
10. **Diagonal Composition** – Use diagonal lines and arrangements to create movement and energy.
11. **Triangular Composition** – Arrange elements into triangular structures to create stability.
12. **Radial Composition** – Arrange elements around a central focal point.

## Core Design Principles

13. **Visual Hierarchy** – Establish the order in which elements attract attention.
14. **Contrast** – Use differences in colour, size, shape, brightness and texture.
15. **Scale and Proportion** – Use relative sizes to communicate importance, distance and relationships.
16. **Alignment** – Position elements in relation to shared lines or axes.
17. **Repetition** – Repeat elements to establish consistency and visual rhythm.
18. **Proximity** – Group related elements together.
19. **Unity** – Make individual elements feel like parts of a cohesive whole.
20. **Variety** – Introduce differences to create interest and avoid monotony.
21. **Rhythm** – Create visual movement through repetition and variation.
22. **Movement** – Direct the viewer's eye through the composition.
23. **Emphasis** – Make particular elements stand out as focal points.
24. **Balance** – Distribute visual weight throughout the composition.
25. **Tension** – Use imbalance, positioning or conflicting elements to create visual interest.
26. **Harmony** – Use complementary elements to create a pleasing overall arrangement.

## Additional Compositional Considerations

27. **Visual Weight** – Understand how colour, contrast, size and complexity influence perceived importance.
28. **Eye Flow** – Control the path the viewer's eyes follow through the composition.
29. **Edge Awareness** – Consider how objects interact with the boundaries of the canvas.
30. **Tangents** – Avoid awkward accidental intersections between elements.
31. **Odd Number Rule** – Groups of three or five elements can feel more natural than evenly paired arrangements.
32. **Simplicity** – Remove unnecessary elements that compete for attention.
33. **Figure-Ground Relationship** – Establish clear relationships between subjects and their surrounding space.
34. **Gestalt Principles** – Use proximity, similarity, continuity, closure and common region to influence how viewers perceive groups of elements.

## Overarching Principle

Composition is about controlling visual attention, creating relationships between elements and communicating a deliberate visual experience.
